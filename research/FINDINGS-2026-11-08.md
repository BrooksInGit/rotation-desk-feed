# Replay findings, 2026-11-08

Replay of all 185 logged plays (Oct 16 to Nov 6) with Alpaca SIP 1-minute bars, the first eligible round-lot trade at
the crossing minute, and OPRA 1-minute option bars, under the live monitor's confirmation rules. Script: `replay.py`.
Two runs: `replay-out.json` (current rules) and `replay-norvol.json` (RVOL recorded, not filtered).

Limits: 3 weeks, one mostly risk-off regime; option fills use the 1-minute option VWAP plus 25% of the play's
recorded spread; scalps without a chosen contract use the nearest traded strike; position limits are ignored;
R = option P&L ÷ the playbook's option stop (28% scalp, 45% swing). Treat every number as directional.

## 1. ORB scalps carried the results; pm_break lost
| run | setup | n | win | avg R | sum R |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| no RVOL filter | orb | 10 | 80% | +1.25 | +12.5 |
| no RVOL filter | pm_break | 37 | 32% | −0.30 | −11.0 |
| current rules | orb | 9 | 78% | +1.15 | +10.3 |
| current rules | pm_break | 14 | 29% | −0.45 | −6.3 |

The twin rule (first to trigger cancels the other) almost always let pm_break win the race because it fires at the
open, so the desk logged 40 ORB plays as `cancelled_pair`. In the replay those cancelled ORBs averaged +1.38R.

## 2. Entries before 9:45 lost; later entries made money
| entry time | n | win | avg R | sum R |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| before 9:45 | 26 | 23% | −0.61 | −15.9 |
| 9:45 or later | 34 | 47% | +0.25 | +8.4 |

## 3. RVOL ≥ 1.5 did not separate winners from losers
| RVOL at entry | n | win | avg R |
|---|---|---|---|
| < 1.0 | 21 | 33% | −0.24 |
| 1.0–1.5 | 17 | 35% | −0.12 |
| 1.5–2.0 | 12 | 33% | −0.39 |
| ≥ 2.0 | 10 | 50% | +0.43 |
Only ≥ 2.0 looked better, on 10 trades. Not enough to change the rule; worth tracking by bucket.

## 4. Swings: almost nothing worked in this tape
13 replayed swing entries, 2 winners. Breakout calls 0/4. Four of the swing plays the desk passed for option
liquidity (ABBV, EBAY, PGR, VLO) would all have hit their stops, so the liquidity filter did not cost anything here.

## 5. Timing cuts both ways
Real-time stops would have cut the desk's MRVL 10/29 loss from −1.22R to −0.54R. But the desk's best trade, AMD 10/22
(+3.72R), was entered late by the hourly check; at real-time precision it would have entered at 9:45 and been stopped
(−1.02R). Faster is not automatically better; the entry-time and setup patterns above matter more than latency.
